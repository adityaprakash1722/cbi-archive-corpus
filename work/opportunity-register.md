# Working Opportunity Register

This is an interim hypothesis register. Nothing here is a recommendation until
the complete corpus, structured data, competitor landscape, buyer, and regulatory
path have been tested.

| ID | Narrow problem hypothesis | Primary buyer | Evidence already observed | Strongest current objection / falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Fraud victims face fragmented, complex reporting routes; late or absent reporting materially reduces recovery. Build a white-label single intake, evidence-preservation, routing, and recovery-status layer for financial providers. | Banks, payment/e-money firms, insurers; potentially a public anti-fraud body | 2026 CBI survey: 38% did not report; 13% recovery without reporting versus 57% after reporting. Oireachtas called for uniform standards, a lead entity, and cross-sector coordination. | Banks may build intake internally; lawful cross-entity data sharing depends on legislation; consumer-facing recovery services face a trust problem and scam risk. |
| C1 | Credit unions need shared specialist capacity to expand mortgage/SME lending while controlling ALM, underwriting, CPC, IT, and DORA risk. Build a non-balance-sheet collaboration and evidence layer. | Irish credit unions, CUSOs, representative bodies | 172 active CUs, 3.7m members, €18.7bn savings versus €7.7bn loans; CBI describes a persistent low loan-to-asset ratio and calls for coordinated strategy and stronger skills/risk management. | CUDA/BizFin, Collaborative Finance, Progress, Metamo/CU-Tech, and a proposed central treasury already address significant parts of the problem; market is concentrated and sales may require sector-wide alignment. |
| O1 | Firms cannot maintain a live, customer-impact-aware map of critical services, ICT dependencies, incidents, concentration, and exit plans. Build a DORA service/dependency evidence graph with regulator-ready exports. | Smaller regulated firms, payments firms, credit unions, fund service providers | CBI reports common DORA embedding gaps, under-reporting of incidents, weak third-party monitoring, vendor lock-in, and cross-industry outages. | DORA software is crowded; a register generator alone is a commodity; deep integrations and assurance liability may overwhelm a startup. |
| Q1 | Financial firms struggle to connect complaints, errors, remediation, vulnerability, and product decisions into demonstrable consumer outcomes. Build consumer-outcome observability and root-cause MI mapped to CPC requirements. | Banks, insurers, investment firms, payments firms, credit unions | CBI sees actual/potential harm from errors and poor customer treatment; 2026 priorities include complaints handling and MI-driven recurring-issue prevention. FSPO banking complaints rose in 2025. | Generic complaint/VoC analytics are abundant; value requires access to sensitive case and product data and proof that existing case-management tools cannot be configured. |
| D1 | Regulatory returns are produced by fragmented processes that create duplication, misclassification, weak review, and recurring data-quality errors. Build data lineage, reconciliation, root-cause, and evidence controls above existing reporting systems. | Regulated firms and reporting service providers | Current CBI work calls data quality an enduring focus and warns against short-term firefighting; historical inspections and current DORA guides show recurring errors. | Mature regulatory-reporting and data-governance vendors exist; the archive may overrepresent consultation complaints and older issues already solved. |
| M1 | Eligible mortgage holders continue to overpay because comparison, document collection, legal transfer, and switching are slow and complex. Build a continuous eligibility monitor and reusable switch pack. | Mortgage brokers/lenders (B2B2C) or consumers | Multiple CBI studies find inertia, comparison difficulty, legal-process delay, and materially lower switching among some groups. | Brokers and switching sites already serve this need; lender economics and conveyancing dependencies may prevent straight-through execution; new CPC prompts may reduce the gap. |
| P1 | Payment/e-money firms lack integrated safeguarding reconciliation, fraud/customer-treatment evidence, and wind-down readiness as scale grows. Build a control and evidence cockpit focused on safeguarding flows and customer-money return. | Ireland-authorised payment and e-money firms | 58 firms processed €702bn in 2025; CBI still finds significant safeguarding deficiencies, weak controls, fraud growth, and inadequate wind-down triggers. | The buyer set is small, large firms have internal systems, and safeguarding/AML vendors are established; scope could become regulated audit or assurance work. |

## Promotion rule

A finalist needs at least one Grade A signal or two independent Grade B signals,
a named buyer with a plausible budget, a narrow MVP that avoids disproportionate
licensing, and a credible answer to the strongest incumbent solution.
