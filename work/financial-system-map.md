# Ireland Financial-System Map (working)

This is a synthesis scaffold, not the final report. Figures below are from the CBI
*Regulatory & Supervisory Outlook 2026* unless noted. They describe entities within
the CBI mandate and should not be added together as if they were comparable balance
sheets, customers or domestic economic activity.

## System shape

## Structural strengths

1. **Ireland is simultaneously a domestic financial system and a global financial
   services hub.** Banking, insurance, payments and funds have large internationally
   oriented components. This creates talent, infrastructure and export capability, but
   domestic consumer outcomes cannot be inferred from aggregate international scale.
2. **Financial resilience is materially stronger than the pre-crisis baseline.** CBI
   describes strong capital/liquidity or solvency positions across core sectors. The
   most acute current gaps are often operational, conduct, data and service quality,
   rather than an immediate system-wide solvency weakness.
3. **The mutual sector has unusually broad reach.** Credit-union membership is roughly
   comparable to Ireland's adult population scale, giving community institutions a
   potential distribution advantage if specialist capacity can be shared safely.
4. **Regulation is moving from rules-on-paper to outcomes in practice.** The revised
   Consumer Protection Code, DORA and individual-accountability regime create clearer
   obligations around governance, support and operational evidence.
5. **Public data is unusually rich but operationally fragmented.** The archive contains
   research, granular statistical datasets, supervisory findings, consultations and
   historical policy material. It is valuable only when provenance and definitions are
   preserved; simple keyword counts would systematically over-weight consultation files.

## Cross-system gaps

### G1 — Handoffs fail at the moment of customer harm

Fraud, complaints, account access and claims frequently cross front-line support,
operations, second-line risk, another institution and sometimes a public body. CBI's
recent guidance and reviews repeatedly emphasize rapid access, issue recognition,
complete resolution, root-cause analysis and customer updates. The gap is less “lack
of another chatbot” than failure to preserve context and accountability across handoffs.

### G2 — Evidence is produced after the fact rather than during the workflow

CBI reports data-quality firefighting, weak incident reporting, uneven complaint QA,
and governance/root-cause gaps. Firms often own systems of record but lack a reliable
event trail linking customer impact, operational cause, action, decision and read-across.
This is a broad problem; a startup needs a narrow event type and buyer to avoid becoming
another generic GRC platform.

### G3 — Scale is present, but capacity is unevenly distributed

Large international groups can build sophisticated controls. Smaller PSPs, credit
unions, intermediaries and specialist lenders face many of the same outcome obligations
without equivalent teams or integration budgets. Shared services can close the gap,
but several credit-union and DORA initiatives already exist, so uncovered workflow
specificity matters more than a general “shared platform” pitch.

### G4 — Fast digital rails magnify slow coordination

Payments execute immediately while reporting, inter-provider communication, recall,
law-enforcement handoff and redress can be fragmented. The planned shared fraud database
addresses intelligence and prevention; public evidence still points to a victim-action
and case-orchestration gap, subject to vendor-scope and legal-basis validation.

### G5 — International success can coexist with domestic concentration and exclusion

Sector scale, ETF leadership and foreign-facing assets are genuine strengths, but they
do not answer whether Irish households and SMEs receive competitive prices, adequate
credit, accessible cash, insurance coverage or effective redress. The final report must
pair international-sector metrics with domestic lending, switching, complaint,
inclusion and regional-access data before making distributional claims.

## Fraud measurement map

The official 2024 measures answer different questions and must remain separate:

| Measure | Value | Meaning / population |
|---|---:|---|
| Fraudulent payment value | €160m | Payment transactions identified as fraudulent in the national release |
| Fraudulent payment volume | 815,000 | Fraudulent transactions, not victims or complaints |
| Online fraud value | €124m (77.4%) | Channel component of fraudulent payment value |
| Final booked loss | €66.4m | Loss allocated/booked after investigation; can occur in a later period |
| Payment/e-money-institution fraudulent payments | €57m | Narrower Irish-resident sector statistic in the Supervisory Outlook |
| FSPO disputed-transaction complaints | 1,297 | Complaints category covering fraud plus missing money, security and unauthorised transactions |

Cards (€45.4m) and credit transfers (€67.7m) reconcile between the granular payment CSV
and rounded release. Suppressed instrument cells and a non-additive published hierarchy
mean the €160m headline cannot be safely reconstructed by summing visible CSV rows.

## Evidence-quality conclusions from the complete archive

1. **Publication volume is not institutional emphasis.** “Payments” matched 1,600
   documents and “complaints/redress” 1,069, but consultation submissions and repeated
   versions inflate counts. Fraud matched 469 documents; 95 were stakeholder
   submissions and 374 were CBI/other institutional classes.
2. **Structured files need semantic QA after parsing.** All 246 CSVs and 489 workbooks
   were readable, yet suppressed values, shifted dates and incomplete headline rows can
   still invalidate naïve aggregation.
3. **The archive contains both evidence and data-collection machinery.** Of 489
   workbooks, 130 are research-supporting data and 120 published-data books, but 112 are
   reporting templates, 72 are applications/forms and 47 are taxonomies/validation
   artifacts. Five XML files are regulatory examples/schemas.
4. **Current authoritative findings outrank keyword saturation.** The startup thesis
   relies on the 2025/2026 Consumer Protection Code, supervisory reviews, fraud research,
   payment statistics and institutional programme records—not the number of search hits.

## Remaining empirical gaps

- Provider-level fraud-case volumes, operating minutes, recall latency and recovery by
  fraud type are not public and require primary buyer research.
- Public sources do not reveal the selected BPFI database vendor's complete private
  feature/interface scope.
- Complaint counts cannot be normalized by provider/customer base from the current
  public aggregate without additional denominators and classification consistency.
- The downloaded tables do not support a robust regional intersection of cash access,
  vulnerability and exclusion without external geospatial/demographic data.
